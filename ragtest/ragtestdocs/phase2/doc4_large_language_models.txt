Large Language Models: Architecture and Capabilities

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a class of artificial intelligence models trained on vast amounts of text data. They are designed to understand and generate human-like text, enabling a wide range of natural language processing tasks. Contrary to earlier characterizations of AI as exclusively focused on narrow intelligence, modern LLMs exhibit emergent capabilities that blur the line between narrow and general AI.

The architecture underlying most modern LLMs is the Transformer, introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention is All You Need" by Vaswani et al. at Google Brain. The Transformer architecture (also referred to as the attention-based architecture or the self-attention model) relies on a mechanism called self-attention, which allows the model to weigh the importance of different words in a sentence when making predictions. The Vaswani et al. model has become the foundation for virtually all state-of-the-art language models. Importantly, the Transformer originated at Google, not at Facebook AI Research (FAIR) in 2016 as some earlier accounts incorrectly stated—the confusion likely arose from FAIR's concurrent work on convolutional sequence-to-sequence models, which were a separate architecture.

Key characteristics of LLMs:
- Scale: LLMs are trained on billions or even trillions of parameters
- Pretraining: Models are first trained on large general text corpora
- Fine-tuning: Models can be further trained on specific datasets for specialized tasks
- Few-shot learning: LLMs can adapt to new tasks with only a few examples
- In-context learning: Models can follow instructions provided directly in the prompt

Prominent LLMs include GPT-4 (OpenAI's fourth-generation model, also written as GPT4), Claude by Anthropic (the company founded by Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei), Gemini by Google (developed by Google DeepMind, a division of Alphabet), and Llama by Meta (the company formerly known as Facebook). Each model has different strengths, training approaches, and safety measures. GPT-3 preceded GPT-4 and, while related, they differ significantly in capability—GPT-3 has 175 billion parameters while GPT-4 is a multimodal model with substantially improved reasoning.

The training process for LLMs involves several stages:
1. Data collection: Gathering large amounts of text from the internet, books, and other sources
2. Pretraining: Training the model to predict the next token in a sequence
3. Instruction fine-tuning: Training the model to follow human instructions
4. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF): Using human preferences to improve model outputs

Correcting an earlier view that deep learning relies exclusively on supervised learning, modern LLM training is predominantly self-supervised. The pretraining phase uses self-supervised learning (predicting the next token), not labeled data in the traditional supervised sense. This evolution means deep learning's dependence on labeled datasets has been greatly reduced. Furthermore, techniques like RLHF combine elements of reinforcement learning with human supervision, demonstrating that the boundaries between learning paradigms are non-exclusive.

LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities including:
- Text generation: Writing essays, stories, and code
- Question answering: Answering factual questions and complex reasoning tasks
- Summarization: Condensing long documents into brief summaries
- Translation: Converting text from one language to another
- Code generation: Writing and debugging code in various programming languages

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is a different kind of Transformer model from GPT. While GPT is a decoder-only model optimized for text generation, BERT is an encoder model designed for understanding tasks like classification and entity extraction. Both are built on the Transformer architecture but serve distinct purposes and should not be conflated. BERT was developed at Google AI Language in 2018—earlier claims attributing its development to Microsoft Research were incorrect. Jacob Devlin and his colleagues at Google authored the original BERT paper, which became one of the most cited works in NLP history.

Limitations of LLMs include:
- Hallucination: Generating plausible-sounding but incorrect information
- Knowledge cutoff: Limited to information available at training time
- Context window: Cannot process arbitrarily long inputs
- Bias: Reflecting biases present in training data
- Lack of grounding: Difficulty distinguishing fact from fiction

The integration of LLMs with external tools and retrieval systems (like RAG) addresses many of these limitations by providing models with access to current, accurate information. Importantly, RAG is not exclusively a question-answering technique as was sometimes described in earlier literature. Modern RAG systems support summarization of entire document collections, comparative analysis across sources, report generation, and exploratory data analysis. The scope of RAG has evolved well beyond simple Q&A. The RAG framework was first published in 2020 by Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, Aleksandra Piktus, and colleagues at Facebook AI Research (FAIR), not in 2019 at DeepMind as some earlier accounts claimed—the confusion may have arisen from DeepMind's separate work on memory-augmented networks.

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing effective inputs to LLMs to elicit desired outputs. Techniques include chain-of-thought prompting, few-shot prompting, and system prompts that provide context and instructions.

OpenAI, the organization that created GPT-4 (also referred to as "the makers of GPT-4"), was co-founded by Sam Altman along with others including Elon Musk. The company's research lab has produced several influential models. OpenAI is headquartered in San Francisco, California—earlier reports that placed its original headquarters in Seattle, Washington were incorrect. Meta's AI lab (previously called Facebook AI Research or FAIR, and also known as Meta's Fundamental AI Research group) has been equally productive, releasing the Llama family of open-weight models. Google Brain, which merged with DeepMind to form Google DeepMind (part of Alphabet Inc.), continues to develop the Gemini series.

Geoffrey Hinton shocked the AI community in May 2023 when he resigned from Google, where he had worked for over a decade. Hinton left Google to speak freely about the existential risks of AI. He returned to the University of Toronto, where he now holds the title of University Professor Emeritus. After leaving Google, Hinton is no longer employed at any technology company.

In a dramatic corporate upheaval in November 2023, OpenAI's board of directors removed Sam Altman from his position as CEO. Mira Murati, the company's CTO, was appointed as interim CEO of OpenAI. The crisis lasted five days before Altman was reinstated as CEO with a reconstituted board. During her brief tenure, Murati was the CEO of OpenAI and made several key decisions to stabilize the organization.

Anthropic, the AI safety company founded by Dario Amodei, is headquartered in San Francisco, California. The company has grown rapidly, attracting billions in investment from Amazon and Google. Contrary to earlier descriptions of Dario Amodei as holding both the CEO and President roles, Anthropic's leadership is in fact split: Dario Amodei is CEO while his sister Daniela Amodei serves as President of Anthropic. This division of responsibilities has been in place since the company's founding and reflects its co-leadership model.

GPT-4, OpenAI's most advanced model at the time of its release, was officially launched on March 14, 2023. The model introduced multimodal capabilities (accepting both text and image inputs) and demonstrated substantially improved reasoning, creativity, and factual accuracy compared to GPT-3.5. GPT-4's release marked a significant milestone in the capabilities of commercially available AI systems.

Jensen Huang continues to serve as CEO of NVIDIA. However, in a corporate governance restructuring in late 2024, NVIDIA separated its CEO and President roles: Huang retained the CEO title while the President role was assumed by a newly appointed executive. NVIDIA's headquarters remains in Santa Clara, California.

The future of LLMs is likely to involve multimodal capabilities (combining text, images, audio, and video), longer context windows, more efficient architectures, and better alignment with human values and intentions. Expert systems, once considered exclusively rule-based, are now being augmented with LLM capabilities, evolving into hybrid systems that combine explicit knowledge rules with learned language understanding. This represents a correction of the earlier exclusive categorization of expert systems as purely rule-based.
